# Attention-head pruning, 2025–2026: evidence review and research direction

**Scope cutoff:** 22 November 2026  
**Search window:** public release or publication from 1 January 2025 through 22 November 2026  
**Evidence set:** 40 distinct papers; versions of the same work are counted once  
**Review type:** structured scoping review using official proceedings, journal/publisher records, and arXiv. This is not a preregistered PRISMA review or an exhaustive database census. Publication dates are reported at the precision exposed by each primary record; when a record only exposed a year, I do not infer a day.

## Executive assessment

Recent literature already covers most broad ideas that might initially sound novel: task-conditioned head selection, causal head scoring, per-question inference-time suppression, dataset-specific head rankings, head interactions, cross-domain pruning in a vision domain, and actual-hardware latency for some structural pruning methods. A paper proposing only “a new importance score,” “causal head scoring,” or “task-aware pruning” would face substantial direct overlap.

The most defensible candidate is narrower: **test whether functional head signatures measured on source models can predict which physically removable heads are safe to remove in a target model from a held-out architecture family and task domain, using no target labels**. Prior work studies cross-dataset transfer within a family or cross-domain pruning in a narrow vision setting, while many recent pruning systems compute a fresh target-model score. I did not verify a 2025–2026 study that establishes label-free transfer of *causal removal utility* across held-out model families. This is a candidate gap, not a novelty claim: it still needs a citation-chain audit and a dedicated search for head correspondence, model merging, and pruning transfer before being called novel.

This direction could support a substantial journal study only if it demonstrates statistically reliable held-out transfer, physical model shrinkage, and measured latency or memory gains. If the evidence is limited to same-family transfer, target labels, or a small accuracy-only pilot, it is more appropriate for a workshop paper. No study can guarantee Q1 acceptance.

## Scope and interpretation rules

“Head pruning” is used inconsistently. I separate five interventions:

1. **Structural removal:** delete complete heads or supported query/key/value head groups from model weights and executable graph. This can shrink parameters and may improve runtime.
2. **Masking or suppression:** leave the checkpoint intact and disable a head for an input, token, or decode step. This changes behavior but does not itself shrink the model.
3. **Learned soft gates or scaling:** reduce a head’s contribution, often with calibration or fine-tuning; whether it compresses the checkpoint depends on whether gates are later folded and removed.
4. **Head-selected adaptation:** choose which heads may update during fine-tuning. This is not inference-time structural pruning.
5. **Head attribution for another target:** use head effects to prune weights or explain behavior. This informs head utility but is not itself a head-pruning method.

The 40 papers below directly remove, mask, gate, rank, transfer, or causally intervene on attention heads. Mixed token/head/FFN methods are included but clearly labeled; their results cannot be attributed to head pruning alone. Token-only pruning and general attention sparsification are excluded, even when they use heads to score tokens.

## Synthesis

### Themes and what the evidence supports

- **Structural compression is now a mature and varied baseline space.** AMP, SlimLLM, HARP, GISP, GRASPrune, Garbage Attention, PUTRI, CoCurve, MP-G, SAP, CAHP and several ViT methods remove heads or head groups. “We propose a head-importance score” is not, by itself, a defensible gap.
- **Task and dataset dependence is directly demonstrated.** SPRINT conditions a mask on the question; ALPS chooses task-adaptation heads; Causal Head Gating reports task-dependent causal roles; the IEICE study finds dataset-specific removable heads; TAHA transfers across plant species with target labels; CLIP head purification learns gates for domain-invariant features across source domains; the tabular study finds schema-dependent scores. A universal fixed head ranking is a weak assumption.
- **Head interactions are no longer an empty space.** Causal Head Gating measures low-modularity task circuits; CAHP selects complementary heads via a graph; CoCurve models joint head/FFN pruning curvature. A new “interaction-aware” score alone would overlap.
- **The optimization target changes the conclusion.** Reasoning work distinguishes answer-only from chain-of-thought targets; bias and backdoor papers show that quality, fairness, safety and task performance can move in different directions. Perplexity or one aggregate score is not a sufficient proxy for all behaviors.
- **Inference-time suppression is not structural compression.** SPRINT, SPIN, HACP, and visual-grounding-guided suppression select heads dynamically, but do not necessarily reduce the checkpoint’s parameter count or produce faster kernels. Report them as behavior interventions, not as compression gains.
- **Hardware evidence is uneven.** HARP reports H100 latency, TAHA reports edge platforms, CoCurve reports physical prefill/memory measurements, and some vision papers report latency. Many others report FLOPs or parameter counts only. FLOPs and masks do not guarantee runtime improvement, especially with GQA, kernel shape, batching and cache behavior.
- **Mixed-granularity studies need careful attribution.** Several combine head, FFN, channel or token pruning. Their total-model gains cannot be credited to head removal without component ablations and matched budgets.

### Replication, conflicts and methodological weaknesses

1. **Importance is not the same as removal utility.** Gradient, magnitude, entropy, attention patterns, causal gates and task losses rank different properties. The target should be evaluated by actual intervention outcomes on held-out examples, not only score correlation.
2. **Selection leakage is a material risk.** If the same validation/test examples choose head ranks, sparsity ratios, thresholds and recovery settings and then report quality, the result is optimistic. The multi-dimensional ViT study describes using evaluation data during the search; a replication should keep a final test set untouched.
3. **Budgets are incomparable.** “50% heads,” “50% parameters,” “50% attention FLOPs” and “50% total FLOPs” differ substantially, especially in GQA and mixed attention/FFN pruning.
4. **Recovery confounds the pruning signal.** Fine-tuning, output-matrix regression, rescaling or distillation can restore performance. Report immediate post-prune and recovered quality separately, and compare equal recovery data/steps.
5. **Uncertainty reporting is inconsistent.** Some studies give several seeds, while abstracts and some tables report a single run or no interval. Seeds should include calibration selection and recovery, not only downstream examples.
6. **Author-stated limitations are missing from many abstracts and records.** For those rows, I have not converted a missing limitations section into a claim; review inferences are labeled separately. Full-text appendix and code audits are still needed before a journal submission.
7. **Publication status matters.** The review includes 32 peer-reviewed papers and eight 2026 arXiv preprints. Preprint numbers are research leads, not settled comparative evidence. One work can have a preprint and a proceedings version; it is counted once.

### Citation-chain boundary and out-of-window context

The 40-paper count is restricted to the requested 2025–2026 window. A 2026 adjacent study, [Causal Attribution Pruning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19350), uses head ablations to guide weight pruning; it is not counted because the compressed objects are weights rather than attention heads. Before claiming novelty, the closest earlier work must also be revisited: [On the Weak Link Between Importance and Prunability of Attention Heads](https://aclanthology.org/2020.emnlp-main.260/), [Know What You Don't Need: Single-Shot Meta-Pruning for Attention Heads](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.03770), [Why Can You Lay Off Heads? Investigating How BERT Heads Transfer](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.07137), and [Merging Text Transformer Models from Different Initializations](https://openreview.net/forum?id=nWnYSLncXa). These are not counted in the 40. They make it especially important to distinguish *matching functional roles* from simply copying same-index rankings or reusing a within-family head mask.

## Three candidate gaps, ranked and stress-tested

### 1. Label-free transfer of causal head-removal utility across model families and task domains

**Question.** Can a functional signature learned from source models predict the effect of physically removing a target model’s head when the target architecture family and target task domain were held out, using unlabeled target inputs but no target labels?

**Why it remains a candidate.** The closest recent papers establish separate pieces: ALPS transfers selected heads across datasets in a Llama-family adaptation setting; TAHA transfers pruning across plant species but uses target labels and stays within ViTs; CLIP head purification learns task/domain gates from labeled source domains; IEICE finds dataset-specific removable heads; Causal Head Gating measures task-dependent causal roles within Llama 3; CAHP and CoCurve model complementarity/interactions using target-model calibration. A 2026 continual-RL preprint also [partitions heads across tasks for sparse fine-tuning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21336), but does not structurally transfer removal utility. None of those results, as checked here, establishes label-free transfer of *causal removal utility* across a held-out architecture family and held-out task domain. Earlier BERT transfer/meta-pruning and model-merging work remain real overlap, so novelty is unconfirmed pending citation chaining.

**Evidence that would support it:** source-derived rankings retain a useful positive association with target causal ablation utility across at least two unseen model families and held-out tasks; the matched transfer beats same-index, source-rank-copy, random, and strong target-unlabeled baselines at the same physical pruning budget; quality loss is within a predeclared noninferiority margin of a target-labeled oracle.

**Evidence that would refute it:** correspondence is unstable across families; only same-family transfer works; the map needs target labels; target-calibrated MP-G, HIES, CAHP, GISP or CoCurve always matches/beats transfer; or the transfer advantage vanishes after equal recovery and latency measurement.

**Assessment.** Novelty: plausible but unverified. Value: high if it reduces target calibration cost while preserving quality and hardware savings. Feasibility: moderate; use open 1–8B checkpoints, shared unlabeled probes and structured head surgery. Compute: pilot on 1–3B models with a single 24–48GB GPU; full study with 7B models likely needs one or more 80GB GPUs and careful batching. Reviewer risk: prior head transfer, architecture mismatch, weak head correspondence, hidden target-label leakage, and overclaiming “zero-shot.” Strong enough for a journal only with a crossed model-family/task study and measured deployment gains.

### 2. Does task-conditioned causal scoring remain reliable under objective and distribution shift?

**Question.** When the source objective uses answer-token loss, chain-of-thought loss, calibration likelihood, fairness, or a safety measure, which target behaviors are preserved after removing heads on held-out task distributions?

**Closest work.** SPRINT performs per-question reasoning masks; RouteHead routes retrieval queries to head subsets; sparse/continual-RL work partitions heads for adaptation; Causal Head Gating measures task-specific causal roles; CLIP head purification learns task/domain gates; HIES/GISP use objective-dependent scores; inclusive-language and backdoor papers optimize particular social/safety outcomes; the reasoning-fragility preprint finds target definition can reverse ranking. These strongly overlap with broad task-aware pruning.

**Support:** a predeclared causal target yields better held-out multi-objective Pareto quality than common scores under distribution shift, and the effect replicates across models/tasks/seeds.

**Refutation:** target loss choice does not materially change pruning rank, or any gain disappears against strong per-target calibration.

**Assessment.** Novelty: moderate and easy to overstate. Value: high for reasoning/safety use. Feasibility: high at small/medium scale. Reviewer risks: too many objectives, hidden benchmark tuning, and a benchmark paper without a general method. Better as a component or a smaller standalone study unless a clear algorithmic principle emerges.

### 3. Does structured head removal buy real deployment efficiency while preserving behavior beyond accuracy?

**Question.** Under a fixed measured latency or memory budget, does physical head pruning preserve reasoning, calibration, fairness and backdoor robustness better than equally fast alternatives?

**Closest work.** HARP reports H100 latency, TAHA reports edge runtime, CoCurve reports prefill and peak memory, while application-specific works study bias, hallucination, backdoors, uncertainty, privacy and mechanistic circuits. The literature rarely evaluates this full set under matched physical speed budgets.

**Support:** an executable structural prune gives repeatable latency/memory savings and preserves a meaningful multi-behavior profile better than equal-latency baselines across at least two hardware targets.

**Refutation:** kernels show no savings, deployment gains are backend-specific, or a smaller dense/quantized alternative is strictly better.

**Assessment.** Novelty: low-to-moderate unless tied to a sharp new objective or measurement protocol. Value: high for deployers. Feasibility: moderate-to-high but costly due hardware/model breadth. Reviewer risks: mixing task families, incomparable hardware, cherry-picked kernels and treating FLOPs as latency. More appropriate as a broad empirical journal study than a small algorithm-only paper.

## Recommended research direction and falsifiable plan

### Research question and hypotheses

**RQ:** Can source-model functional signatures plus source-side causal removal effects rank structurally removable attention heads in an unseen target model without target labels, and improve quality at equal physical serving budgets over copying source ranks and strong target-unlabeled pruning methods?

- **H1, ranking transfer:** On held-out model families and task domains, transferred head utility has positive rank correlation with target post-intervention utility and exceeds same-index/source-rank baselines. Estimate Spearman’s rho with task/model-family cluster bootstrap confidence intervals.
- **H2, quality at budget:** At fixed 20%, 30% and 40% attention-head FLOP budgets, label-free transfer retains more held-out target quality than random, same-index, source-rank-copy, and strong target-unlabeled baselines. Predeclare task-specific noninferiority margins.
- **H3, calibration advantage:** At zero target labels, the transferred method approaches a target-labeled oracle while using less target calibration than direct target-scoring methods. The oracle is diagnostic, not a fair deployment baseline.
- **H4, actual efficiency:** Physically sliced checkpoints produce measured lower latency or memory on named hardware; theoretical FLOPs alone do not count as an efficiency result.

### Proposed method

1. **Define heads and compatible structural units.** Treat MHA heads and GQA query/KV groups separately. Do not assume that head index, layer position or head count is aligned across architectures.
2. **Build a shared, label-free probe corpus.** Use public unlabeled text that does not overlap with task test sets or known benchmark training examples. Include general language and domain-diverse prompts. Record corpus provenance and deduplicate.
3. **Extract functional signatures.** For each head, record normalized residual-stream changes, output/logit-distribution changes under ablation, activation summary statistics and response patterns over the shared probes. Compare signatures at the level of normalized observable effects, not raw hidden coordinates.
4. **Measure source causal utility.** On source-only labeled train/calibration tasks, physically or functionally ablate a head and measure task-specific utility change. Keep source training, model selection and calibration disjoint.
5. **Match source to target.** Use a constrained bipartite/optimal-transport assignment over functional signatures; allow unmatched heads and report match confidence. Learn the source-side relation from multiple source models, then map the resulting utility distribution to each target head. Do not force one-to-one matches when architectures differ.
6. **Use uncertainty-aware abstention.** If a target head has low-confidence or conflicting matches, abstain from transferring its rank and fall back to a target-unlabeled score. Compare this hybrid with transfer-only and target-score-only variants.
7. **Prune and physically slice.** Remove the selected head/group from Q/K/V/O projections and runtime configuration; verify logits against an equivalent masked model before benchmarking. Recover only under a fixed, disclosed data/step budget and report pre- and post-recovery quality.

The method’s contribution is not “optimal transport” alone. It is the testable transfer protocol, the causal target, uncertainty/abstention, held-out family and task design, and evidence of physical efficiency. If a simpler signature mapping performs equally well, prefer the simpler method.

### Models, tasks and data

**Pilot:** use one encoder family and two small decoder families to validate head surgery and signature extraction (for example BERT/RoBERTa and GPT-2/OPT-sized models). Pilot only establishes feasibility; it cannot support the cross-family claim.

**Confirmatory LLM study:** at least four open decoder families with materially different attention layouts, such as Llama, Mistral, Qwen and Gemma, at 1–8B scales. Hold out complete families during transfer evaluation, not random checkpoints from the same family. Include standard language modeling, instruction following, reasoning, code or domain shift. Choose exact available checkpoints only after licensing and memory feasibility are verified. A vision extension is optional and should be analyzed as a separate domain, not pooled with LLM evidence.

**Split discipline:** source labels may train source utility models and choose source hyperparameters; target inputs may be unlabeled calibration only; target labels and final test sets remain sealed until the mapping and prune budget are frozen. Keep benchmark test examples out of the shared probe corpus. Use separate source train, source validation, target unlabeled probe, target validation for no supervised choices, and target test splits. Report overlap checks, prompt templates and any model-training contamination uncertainty.

### Baselines and fair comparison

- Random head/group removal, stratified by layer.
- Same-index and same-layer source-to-target copying.
- Direct source rank transfer without correspondence mapping.
- Magnitude/MP-G, gradient/HIS/HIES, Greedy-Gnorm, HARP and CAHP where architecture-compatible.
- GISP and GRASPrune for global structural budgets; CoCurve for head/FFN interaction-aware pruning.
- Target-unlabeled direct scoring, with its calibration compute reported.
- Target-labeled oracle ranking as an upper bound only.
- Dense model, physically pruned model without recovery, and equally budgeted recovery version.
- Equal-parameter, equal-attention-FLOP, and equal-measured-latency comparisons reported separately.

No baseline should receive more calibration labels, recovery steps or validation tuning than the proposed method without documenting the difference.

### Statistical analysis and measurements

- Use at least five independent calibration/probe subsampling seeds per model/task. If recovery fine-tuning is used, use at least five recovery seeds for the pilot and confirmatory subset. Fixed pretrained checkpoints are not independent pretraining seeds; state this explicitly.
- Use paired bootstrap over examples and a hierarchical bootstrap or mixed-effects model over tasks and model families. Report effect sizes and 95% intervals, not only p-values. Correct the family of primary comparisons (for example Holm correction) and pre-register primary outcomes.
- Primary metrics: target-task quality at fixed physical head-removal budget; Spearman rank correlation between transferred utility and target ablation utility; latency or memory at matched quality.
- Secondary metrics: perplexity, reasoning accuracy, calibration/error, robustness under domain shift, parameter count, attention FLOPs, peak memory, energy if measurable, and mapping/calibration wall time.
- Profile batch size, prompt/context length, prefill and decode separately, median and tail latency, warm-up, kernel/library versions, quantization, cache policy, GPU/CPU model and power setting. Validate the physically pruned implementation against the masked reference before timing.

### Ablations, negative criteria and staged go/no-go

**Ablations:** signature components; assignment method; one-to-one versus partial matching; confidence threshold and abstention; number/diversity of source models; source-task count; target-label budget 0/1/5/10%; target-unlabeled probe size; MHA versus GQA; removal budget; recovery/no recovery; source and target objective; same-family versus held-out-family transfer.

**Negative criteria set before the confirmatory run:**

- No positive rank correlation after family/task clustering, or no advantage over direct source-rank transfer.
- The method is consistently worse than target-unlabeled MP-G/HIES/CAHP/GISP/CoCurve at equal budget.
- Any quality gain requires target labels, benchmark-test access, or larger recovery budgets than baselines.
- The pruned executable has no repeatable measured latency or memory advantage on the named target hardware.
- Gains appear only on one task, one family, or one probe corpus and fail on the held-out confirmatory set.

**Stage 0 — literature and implementation audit:** finish forward/backward citation trails for ALPS, TAHA, IEICE, Causal Head Gating, CAHP, HARP, CoCurve and earlier head-transfer/model-alignment work. Verify code and check whether an existing method already transfers causal head utility across model families. **Go** only if the exact question remains unresolved.

**Stage 1 — surgery and signature pilot:** validate exact structural head removal against mask equivalence; test matching across two model families. **Go** only if signatures are reproducible and transfer beats same-index and direct source-rank copying on held-out examples.

**Stage 2 — locked cross-family study:** freeze method and thresholds; evaluate at least two held-out families and held-out task domains with labels sealed during selection. **Go** only if primary ranking and quality hypotheses meet their predeclared intervals and noninferiority margins.

**Stage 3 — deployment evidence:** profile physically pruned artifacts on one accelerator and one resource-constrained CPU/edge platform or second accelerator. **Go to journal writing** only if quality gains coexist with measured deployment benefit and independent artifact reproduction.

### Minimum credible journal evidence and remaining risks

A credible submission needs: a verified 2025–2026 and earlier citation chain; at least four model families with fully held-out family splits; multiple target task domains; target labels sealed during pruning; strong, current baselines; matched budgets and recovery; uncertainty and seed analysis; verified structural checkpoints; actual runtime/memory on at least two hardware environments; open code/configs and legal data references; and a discussion of failures and negative results. The result should be independently reproducible.

**What would force a pivot:** if label-free transfer fails but small unlabeled target calibration succeeds, pivot to uncertainty-aware calibration-cost reduction, and frame the negative transfer result honestly. If only same-family transfer works, narrow the claim to within-family checkpoint transfer. If no actual latency benefit appears, stop describing the method as efficient and reconsider whether the quality/interpretability contribution is sufficient.

**Journal positioning:** target a reputable Q1 journal only after the evidence is complete, verify the journal’s current scope and quartile at submission time, and do not select a journal based on quartile alone. Q1 acceptance cannot be guaranteed.

## Search transparency and verification notes

- Publication metadata and venue status were checked against primary proceedings/publisher pages or arXiv records linked in each row. Preprints are explicitly labeled; preprint and venue versions are not double-counted.
- Full-text limitations are cited by section/page where verified. Where only an abstract or metadata record was inspected, the row says so; any further concern is labeled as an inference rather than attributed to the authors.
- Several publisher pages exposed only abstracts in this review environment. Model size, split, code, data, seeds and exact baseline details marked as needing extraction must be verified from the full text and appendices before implementation.
- Borderline recent work screened but not counted in the 40 includes [The Model Knows Where to Look](https://aclanthology.org/2026.aacl-long.152/) and [RouteHead](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.24608), because they suppress/select heads for inference tasks rather than structurally compressing the head inventory; and [Ranking and Partitioning of Attention Heads for Sparse and Continual Reinforcement Learning Fine-tuning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21336), because it selects/partitions parameters for adaptation rather than pruning at inference. They remain relevant adjacent evidence and are discussed in the synthesis/gap stress-test.
- Search services are fallible and the review is time-bounded. The 40-paper corpus is a verified working set, not a claim of exhaustive coverage. A final journal manuscript should export database search queries and dates, add backward/forward citation screening, and record exclusions in a PRISMA-style flow.
- No new experiment was run for this review. The proposed direction is a research plan, not a claim that transfer or performance gains have been observed.
